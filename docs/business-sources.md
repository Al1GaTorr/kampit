## Category-scale evidence, with its limits

Duolingo reported approximately **13.1 million paid subscribers at September 30, 2026**, compared with 11.5 million a year earlier. Its definition excludes free trials and non-paying family-plan members. [Duolingo Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, primary SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026069692/duol-20260930.htm).

This shows substantial paid consumer-learning demand in an adjacent business. It is a global company metric: it is not a US age-25–45 market count, Kampit's TAM/SAM, a pool Kampit can capture, or a conversion forecast. Do not use an assumed percentage of Duolingo subscribers as an evidenced Kampit market size.

## Slide-ready proposed starting customer and value

**Target hypothesis:** US working adults aged 25–45 who repeatedly start an after-work skill goal and stop practising. Begin with a narrow cohort willing to practise an authored language, coding or writing task in short daily sessions; interview and observe them before treating this as a validated segment.

**Value hypothesis:** make the next small practice session easier to start and worth returning to, with a personal daily plan, visible saved work and a shared weekly scoreboard. Points represent participation under transparent rules; they do not certify mastery.

**Paid hypothesis:** keep private friend competitions free; test **$8 per month** for access to open worldwide competitions and persistent competitive progress. The proposed value is fresh competition without having to recruit all opponents personally. Existing Prime activation is clearly labeled no-charge demo access; there is no payment processor or revenue. The $8 proposal is an experiment, not a publicly validated product price, and Focusmate's annual-equivalent $8 is a different billing commitment.

## Business-potential arithmetic — scenario only

At a hypothetical $8/month, annual revenue per continuously paying user is $96 before discounts, taxes and churn. Therefore:

| Average equivalent paying users for a full year | Illustrative ARR |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1,000 | $96,000 |
| 10,000 | $960,000 |
| 100,000 | $9.6 million |
| 1,041,667 | approximately $100 million |

Formula: $100,000,000 / ($8 × 12) = 1,041,666.67 paying-user equivalents. This is a reverse calculation of the scale required for the rubric's long-term $100M ARR ambition. It is not a forecast, achieved result or researched market size.

An illustrative unit-economics test assumes $10 CAC, 80% gross margin and six paid months per customer: contribution LTV = $8 × 0.8 × 6 = **$38.40**; LTV/CAC = **3.84**; contribution payback = $10 / $6.40 = **1.56 months**. Every input is unvalidated. This simplified model excludes fixed operating costs and is not a profitability claim. At three paid months, the same assumed inputs produce LTV $19.20 and ratio 1.92; churn can undermine the model quickly.

## Validation and growth proposal, not completed research

Recruit an initial US working-adult cohort through relevant learning communities and individual creator referrals. Private friend invitations are an implemented product mechanism; the acquisition efficiency of that loop is unknown. Track onboarding-to-first-completion, accepted invites, weekly practice returns and actual paid-offer acceptance. Predefine denominators and observation windows, and report actual results separately from the two existing synthetic QA accounts.

Test whether fresh open competition adds enough value beyond free friend groups to support $8/month. If users will not return or pay, change the value proposition before scaling paid acquisition. No enterprise contract, B2B pivot, fabricated testimonials or AI-grading claim is needed for this B2C submission.
